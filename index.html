Star Trek keeps time twice. There is the date it reached us — a premiere, an opening weekend — and the stardate in the captain's log. The two run in different directions, at different speeds, in three incompatible numbering systems, and a good third of the franchise gave up on stardates entirely. Every film and every season of every series is here. Pick a clock and watch them rearrange.

    Every season is entered at its first episode: the date that episode first aired, and the in‑universe dating of that episode. Stardates are quoted from log entries spoken on screen — where a premiere never states one, the entry is drawn hollow and dashed rather than guessed at, and a grey marker means the production never used stardates at all. A year shown as c. is inferred from the surrounding story rather than stated. Film release dates are original US theatrical premieres, except Section 31, which went straight to Paramount+. The short-film anthologies Short Treks and Very Short Treks are left out: their instalments have no shared season chronology to plot.
